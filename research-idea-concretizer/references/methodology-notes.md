# Methodology Notes

## 1. Why expand before evaluating

Early ranking causes premature convergence. A vague seed often contains several latent interpretations. Expansion should therefore precede strong feasibility or novelty filtering.

## 2. Why the MRI tuple is useful

`Problem + Gap + Hypothesis + Method + Evaluation + Contribution` prevents common failure modes:

- topic masquerading as idea;
- solution-first reasoning;
- unfalsifiable claims;
- no credible comparator;
- engineering output without scientific contribution.

## 3. Why orthogonality matters

Generating many wordings is not generating many ideas. Research diversification requires variation in the central scientific question or causal claim.

## 4. Why falsification is mandatory

A research idea should specify what evidence would make its central claim unsupported. This sharply distinguishes scientific hypothesis generation from product brainstorming.

## 5. Why Agent-specific checks are separate

Agent systems can appear novel because orchestration is complex. The meaningful scientific question is whether the system must make adaptive, state-dependent decisions that cannot be adequately predetermined by a static workflow.

## 6. Why novelty claims are status-labeled

Literature coverage is never perfect. `verified / partially verified / unverified` prevents a candidate gap from being presented as established fact before adequate searching.

## 7. Portfolio rather than single winner

Different research directions trade off novelty, feasibility, validation burden, and upside. A portfolio preserves optionality and is a better input to downstream formal evaluation.
