# Synthesis-driven storyboarding

Typical 14-slide technical narrative (reallocate to suit project):

1. **Editorial cover** — important question and thesis (not a paper title dump).
2. **Key tension** — one sharp defensible statement and why the existing literature is difficult to interpret.
3. **Scope / definitions** — included/excluded mechanisms, endpoint distinctions and review type.
4. **Taxonomy** — clear operational category boundaries; overlap allowed; visibly label authors' categories.
5. **Mechanism grammar** — input, interaction, output; show what information crosses modalities.
6. **Evaluation criteria** — independent evidence questions; don't use decorative success checkmarks.
7. **Comparability contract** — what conditions a cross-paper claim needs.
8. **Comparison matrix** — task, resolution, supervision, mechanistic role, evidence and limitations.
9. **Claim/evidence map** — original support vs inference vs open verification, with traceable IDs.
10. **Multi-track evolution** — chronological tracks; draw technical-dependency arrows only when sourced.
11. **Conflicts and boundary cases** — alternative interpretations, measurement artifacts and unknowns.
12. **Research agenda** — experiments that discriminate competing explanations, not generic future-work wishes.
13. **Synthesis** — separate grounded conclusions, author proposals and unresolved issues.
14. **Closing** — the one decision the audience can legitimately take away.

Prefer visuals over enumeration. Use one primary diagram/table per slide. A review audience needs intellectual structure, not source-order recitation. Every assertive slide headline must be at or below the support level of its claims.

Layout routing: `big_idea` for a thesis; `scope` for scope/evidence types; `taxonomy` for concepts; `mechanisms` for 2–3 functional roles; `lanes` for orthogonal lenses; `comparability` for control conditions; `matrix` for condensed studies/conditions; `evidence_map` for provenance/uncertainty; `timeline` for tracked evolution; `debate` for conflicting interpretations; `agenda` for actionable tests; `synthesis` for qualified takeaways; `figure` for source images. `cover` and `closing` frame the talk.
