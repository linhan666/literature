# Presentation paths by review type

**Narrative-critical review** (default unless a genuine protocol says otherwise): central argument, explicit criteria, provenance-aware claims, tensions and confounders, proposed taxonomy, boundary cases, research agenda. Do not invent a PRISMA workflow or claim exhaustive coverage.

**Technical survey:** organize by mechanism primitives, inputs/outputs, objective/loss, inference budget, provenance of implementation (paper/code), comparability axes, benchmark provenance and failure modes. Distinguish implementations sharing a name but not verified as technically related.

**Scoping review:** describe the documented question, scope, search/screening dates, actual databases/query strings and included records. Counts must be auditable. Prefer evidence distribution and research gaps; avoid efficacy meta-claims unsupported by the review design.

**Systematic review/meta-analysis:** only use these labels if documented protocol and methods support them. Show real eligibility, duplicate removal, screening totals, risk-of-bias/applicability assessment and effect synthesis where appropriate. Do not make up study counts, PRISMA boxes, pooled estimates, heterogeneity, confidence intervals, forest plots, or certainty ratings.

**Journal club / reading group:** central claim → method mechanism → strong and weak evidence → methodological confounders → unresolved questions → implications for local work. A single-paper journal club is not a comprehensive field review.

**Thesis background:** problem landscape → assumptions → method taxonomy → evidence status → rationale for a thesis-sized open question; cite all factual precedent and distinguish novelty claims from confirmed gaps.

Defaults: 12–16 slides, 15–20 minutes, technical colleagues, one insight/slide; scale to audience. Reserve appendix for comprehensive paper matrices and citations; no tiny reference walls on primary slides.
